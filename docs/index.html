Booking, and the email that has to go out exactly once

A multi-role scheduling app — client, provider and admin, each with their own dashboard, over one slot engine. Two things in it are harder than they look: turning recurring weekly hours into a live list of free slots without ever offering the same one twice, and sending five kinds of automatic email in a way that survives overlapping scans, a double-clicked button and several workers at once.

Repository Open app (local)

What a free slot is, and what it has to survive

calendar_logic.py turns a provider's recurring weekly hours, their one-off blocked dates and their existing bookings into the list of times a client may actually pick, for any date.

    Five messages, each sent exactly once

    All five go out without anyone pressing a button. The difficulty is not sending them; it is not sending them twice.

    Delivery happens on a background thread, so a slow mail server never slows down a booking.
    WhenWho gets mailed

    Nobody is mailed twice

    A unique index on email_log (appointment_id, kind, recipient) is the source of truth rather than a flag somebody remembers to set, so overlapping reminder scans, a double-clicked button and several web workers running at once all collapse to one message.

    Nothing blocks, and nothing is lost

    mailer.send() writes a row and hands the message to a worker thread; the booking request returns immediately. A refused SMTP connection is a logged failed row rather than a 500, and the next reminder scan picks it up again.

    Coffee chats: an email that produces a booking

    The reverse of the usual direction. You send someone an invite, they click the link, they see your real availability, they pick a time — and they never make an account.
